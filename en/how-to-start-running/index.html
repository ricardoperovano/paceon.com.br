For beginners

How to start running from scratch (without getting hurt or quitting)

Starting to run is easy; keeping going is the hard part. Most people quit in the first three weeks for one reason: they ran too fast, it hurt, and running became punishment. This guide is about doing it differently.

First week: alternate running and walking

Start with 5 minutes of walking, then 6 blocks of 1 minute running very slowly and 2 minutes walking, and finish with 5 minutes of walking. That's 28 minutes. Do it three times a week on alternate days. If it feels too easy, great: that's how the first week should feel.

Running "very slowly" means being able to speak full sentences. For most beginners that's a pace between 7:30 and 9:00 per kilometer — just a bit faster than a brisk walk. There's no such thing as too slow at the start; there's only too fast.

Progression over the first 8 weeks

Every week or two, increase the running time and reduce the walking: 2 minutes to 1, then 4 to 1, then 10 continuous minutes, then 20. By week eight, most people can run 30 minutes without stopping — the equivalent of 4 or 5 km. If a week gets hard, repeat it. The plan adjusts to you, not the other way around.

Don't increase total volume (minutes of running per week) by more than 10% from one week to the next, and do a lighter week every three or four. That rhythm seems conservative and that's exactly why it works: tendons and bones take months to adapt, and they're what gets hurt when haste wins.

What you need (and what you don't)

You need: cushioned running shoes in your size (half a size bigger than your everyday shoes), seamless socks and clothes that don't overheat. You don't need: a GPS watch, a heart-rate monitor, gels, supplements. All of that can come later, if you want. A phone in your pocket already records distance and time.

Normal pain and pain that isn't

Muscle stiffness in the two days after a run is normal and goes away with the warm-up. Localized pain at a point on a bone, pain that gets worse over the run or pain that changes your stride aren't normal: stop for a few days and, if it persists, seek professional assessment. Before starting any exercise program, a medical check-up is recommended — especially if you have a history of heart or joint problems or are overweight.

How not to quit

Put the runs in your calendar as appointments. Run on the same days and times for a month — habit is built by repetition, not motivation. Log every run: watching the streak grow is one of the strongest motivators there is. And don't compare your beginning with someone else's middle.

Frequently asked questions

I'm overweight. Can I start running?

In most cases, yes, starting with run/walk alternation and with a medical check-up first. The slower progression protects the joints while the body adapts.

Should I run every day?

No. Three times a week on alternate days is ideal at the start. The rest day between runs is part of the training.

How long until I can run 5K?

With three runs a week, most people reach 5 km — or 30 continuous minutes — in 8 to 10 weeks.
