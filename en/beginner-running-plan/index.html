Getting started

Beginner running plan: from zero to your first 5K in 8 weeks

Beginners don't need a complex plan: they need one they can stick to. Three short runs a week, a pace at which you can talk, and a small increase every week. In 8 weeks most people run 5 km without stopping.

What the beginner running plan looks like

The first weeks alternate running and walking. Week 1: 6 blocks of 1 minute running and 2 walking, three times a week on non-consecutive days. Week 2: 2 minutes running, 2 walking. Each week the running time grows a little and the walking shrinks, until week 7, when you run 20 minutes straight, and week 8, when you close out the 5 km.

The pace is conversational: you can say a full sentence without gasping. For beginners that's usually between 7:30 and 9:00 per kilometer, and that's fine. The gain comes from consistency, not speed.

The three mistakes that make beginners quit

Running too fast in the first days, which turns training into suffering and brings pain. Training on consecutive days without giving the body its recovery day, when tendons and bones haven't yet adapted to impact. And increasing volume all at once instead of 10% a week. A beginner plan exists precisely to prevent all three.

What you need, and what you don't

You need comfortable running shoes, clothes that don't bother you and water. You don't need a GPS watch, supplements or a pace goal. If you have your phone with Strava or any running app, runs come into PaceOn on their own; if you don't, you can log by hand in ten seconds.

How PaceOn builds your beginner plan

You answer four questions — how many days you have, whether you've run before and what your goal is — and get an 8-week plan with running and walking dosed for your starting point. Every workout comes explained in plain language. After each run, the following week is reviewed: if you missed a day or felt it was too heavy, the plan steps back instead of piling up.

Frequently asked questions

I've never run. Can I follow the plan?

Yes. It starts with 1 minute of running and 2 of walking. If that's still heavy, the plan starts with walking only and introduces running when the body is ready.

How many times a week should a beginner run?

Three, on non-consecutive days. It's the minimum for the body to adapt and the maximum tendons and joints can handle in the first two months.

Should I worry about pace?

Not in the first two months. The only pace that matters is conversational. Pace becomes a topic once you already run 5 km comfortably.

I felt pain. Should I continue?

Mild muscle soreness the next day is normal. Localized pain that gets worse while running or changes how you land isn't: stop and seek professional assessment. PaceOn doesn't diagnose or prescribe anything health-related.
