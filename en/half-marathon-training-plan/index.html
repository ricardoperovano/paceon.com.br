How the week is organized

A plan isn't a list of runs: every workout has a role.

Base phase

Aerobic volume and consistency, with little intensity and plenty of regularity.

Build phase

Intervals and tempo runs come in while volume keeps rising in a controlled way.

Peak phase

Specificity: longer long runs and race-pace blocks to lock in the target pace.

Taper

Two weeks of reduced volume while keeping intensity, to arrive rested without losing rhythm.

Costly mistakes

  • Increasing the long run by more than 2 km from one week to the next.
  • Running the long run at race pace — it should be comfortable.
  • Cutting down weeks to "save time".
  • Debuting shoes, gels or clothing on race day.

Common questions

Do I need to run 21 km before the race?

No. Most plans top out at a 16–18 km long run. The rest comes from adrenaline and a well-executed taper.

How much preparation time do I need?

For someone already running 10 km regularly, 12 to 16 weeks is the most common window. Beginners should consolidate the 10K first.

How do I set a time goal?

A good estimate starts from a recent result at another distance. The app computes a goal consistent with your current performance and builds a pacing strategy by segment.
