How it works

How the AI running coach works

From your first run to race day: five steps, and the plan becomes yours.

01

Connect your runs

Connect Strava in a few taps and we import your recent history. Garmin, Health Connect, Apple Health, Polar, Coros and Suunto are next. Connecting is free on every plan and import is unlimited.

02

Choose your goal

Start running, 5K, 10K, half, marathon, improve a time or maintain fitness. If you have a race booked, periodization is built up to its date.

03

Get your plan

The AI builds your routine from your history, your pace, your availability and your level. Before it becomes a plan, everything goes through a rules engine: controlled volume progression, never two hard workouts in a row, periodic down weeks and a taper before the race.

04

Run

Keep using your usual watch or app. As soon as the activity reaches the platform, it lands here, gets normalized, deduplicated and matched to the workout that was planned.

05

Your plan evolves

We compare planned and actual, look at effort, pace and consistency — and adjust what comes next. Every adjustment carries the "AI adjustment" label and explains the reason.

What the AI does — and what it doesn't

The AI suggests; the system decides. Every suggestion goes through deterministic safety rules before it becomes a workout. The AI Coach only answers about running, training, races, pace, recovery and performance — it doesn't diagnose or prescribe anything health-related.

The rules every workout has to pass

The artificial intelligence proposes the periodization, but no suggestion becomes a workout without going through a deterministic rules engine. These are the rules any coach would apply: weekly volume progression is capped by your level (5% for beginners, up to 10% for those who have trained for years); the long run never exceeds a third of the week's volume and never grows more than 2 km at once; there are never two hard workouts on consecutive days; every four weeks a down week comes in, with about 30% less volume; and volume drops before the race, in the taper.

When a suggestion breaks a rule, it's adjusted or discarded before it reaches you. That's why the plan is explainable: every correction is recorded with its reason — "long run capped at +2 km over the previous one", "down week inserted to respect the recovery cycle". The AI never writes directly to your plan.

What happens after you run

The activity arrives from Strava within seconds, from a GPX, FIT or TCX file exported from your watch, or from logging it manually in four taps. Whichever the path, it's normalized, checked against duplicates and matched to the workout planned for that day.

Then comes the comparison: planned distance against distance run, pace range against actual pace, target effort against how it felt, per-kilometer splits and heart rate when available. That reading is computed, not written by AI — it's immediate, free and the same for everyone. Pro and Performance subscribers can additionally request an AI Coach analysis of what went well, what deserves attention and what to do over the next days.

When the week doesn't go to plan

An easy run that came out 40 seconds per kilometer faster than your range shows up as an alert, because an easy run that's too fast collects its price on the hard day. Logged pain reduces the load of the next workout. Three runs in a row at high effort weigh on next week's decisions. None of this asks anything of you: the plan reorganizes itself and shows what changed.

The "AI adjustment" label accompanies every automatic change, with the sentence explaining why. If you disagree, you can drag a workout to another day, change the distance on the card itself or request a full review of the week.

The vocabulary of your plan

Long run, tempo run, intervals, taper, RPE, heart-rate zone: every term that shows up in a workout has a "?" next to it that opens a plain-language explanation on the spot. The same vocabulary is gathered in the running glossary, and the blog goes deeper into each type of stimulus with a number, a reason and what to do about it.
